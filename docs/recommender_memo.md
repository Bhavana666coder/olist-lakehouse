# Project 5: Olist Product Recommender

## Objective
Compare a global product-popularity baseline with an
Alternating Least Squares (ALS) collaborative filtering model
using the Brazilian Olist e-commerce dataset.

## Data
- Dataset: Olist Brazilian E-Commerce
- Customer identifier: `customer_unique_id`
- Product identifier: `product_id`
- Interaction: a customer's purchase of a product
- Framework: Apache Spark and PySpark
- Execution: Spark on YARN
- Storage: HDFS and Parquet

## Data Preparation
1. Join order items with orders and customers.
2. Keep eligible purchase records from delivered orders with valid
   purchase timestamps.
3. Aggregate customer-product interactions and retain the latest
   interaction timestamp for each customer-product pair.
4. Filter customers with fewer than two distinct products.
5. Filter products purchased by fewer than two distinct customers.
6. Split each eligible customer's interactions chronologically:
   earlier interactions are used for training, and the latest
   interaction is held out for testing.
7. Store the training and test datasets in HDFS under
   `/datalake/curated/recommender_interactions/`.

## Recommendation Methods

### Method 1: Popularity Baseline
Recommend products with the highest purchase counts in the training
data. Previously purchased products are excluded from each user's
recommendations.

### Method 2: ALS Collaborative Filtering
Use Spark MLlib ALS with implicit feedback.

Configuration:
- Rank: 10
- Regularization parameter: 0.1
- Alpha: 10
- Maximum iterations: 10
- Nonnegative factors: enabled
- Cold-start strategy: drop
- Random seed: 42

## Evaluation
Both methods were evaluated against the held-out product interaction
for eligible test customers using:
- Hit@10
- Precision@10
- NDCG@10
- Catalog coverage

The metrics below are the actual values printed by the Spark evaluation
job. Percentage equivalents are included for readability.

## Results

| Metric | ALS | Popularity |
|---|---:|---:|
| Hit@10 | 0.016999 (1.70%) | 0.029015 (2.90%) |
| Precision@10 | 0.001700 (0.17%) | 0.002902 (0.29%) |
| NDCG@10 | 0.007636 (0.76%) | 0.013634 (1.36%) |
| Catalog coverage | 0.057225 (5.72%) | 0.005352 (0.54%) |

### Interpretation
- The popularity baseline achieved higher Hit@10, Precision@10, and
  NDCG@10 in this experiment.
- ALS achieved greater catalog coverage, recommending a broader range
  of products from the training catalog.
- ALS coverage was approximately 10.7 times the popularity baseline's
  coverage.
- The measured results suggest that, under this dataset preparation,
  chronological split, and model configuration, popularity was the
  stronger method on the three ranking-accuracy metrics.
- These results describe this experiment only. They do not establish
  that popularity will outperform ALS on every dataset or in production.

## Conclusion
The popularity baseline performed better than ALS on all three
ranking-accuracy metrics in the current evaluation. ALS offered
substantially greater catalog coverage, indicating more variety in the
products recommended.

For a simple initial recommender where ranking accuracy on this test
split is the priority, popularity is the stronger candidate based on
these measurements. ALS remains worth investigating if product
discovery and recommendation diversity are important, but its
configuration and candidate-generation process need further evaluation.

## Practical Recommendation
Use the popularity baseline as the initial offline benchmark and
candidate for a controlled online experiment. Before deployment:
1. Verify the data-preparation counts and evaluation implementation.
2. Check recommendation-list lengths and how products absent from
   training are handled.
3. Evaluate performance across customer groups and product categories.
4. Compare additional ALS configurations before concluding that the
   current ALS setup is unsuitable.
5. Validate outcomes with an online experiment before making a
   production decision.

## Proposed A/B Test

### Design
- **Control:** The existing product-discovery experience.
- **Treatment:** Product recommendations generated by the popularity
  baseline, after offline validation.
- **Randomization:** Assign eligible users randomly and persistently
  to one group.
- **Duration:** Run for at least two full weeks, then reassess whether
  the sample size and statistical power are sufficient. Extend the test
  if required.
- **Primary metric:** Click-through rate on recommended products.
- **Secondary metrics:** Add-to-cart rate, purchase conversion rate,
  and revenue per eligible user.
- **Guardrail metrics:** Page latency, error rate, product availability,
  and cancellation/return rate.

### Decision Criteria
Compare treatment and control using a predefined analysis plan and
appropriate statistical uncertainty estimates. Adopt the treatment only
if it improves the primary metric without unacceptable deterioration
in guardrail metrics. Offline Hit@10, Precision@10, NDCG@10, and catalog
coverage should remain useful diagnostic measures, but they do not
guarantee an online business improvement.

No online A/B test has been conducted as part of the current experiment.
